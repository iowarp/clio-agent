# Evidence and citation integrity

Maintain a claim-to-source map. A search result is discovery metadata, never
evidence. A material sourced claim must be supported by successfully fetched
content, cited beside the claim, and bounded to the source's population,
conditions, material state, definitions, and uncertainty.

Prefer primary research, standards, official documentation, datasets, and
authoritative technical reports. Reviews can identify terminology and primary
sources but do not automatically replace them. Preserve paper title, authors,
venue, date/year, DOI or stable identifier, authoritative landing page or readable
document URL, and whether the evidence was full text or abstract only.

Give every attempted source exactly one disposition:

- `USED_AND_CITED`: fetched content supports or changes the synthesis;
- `READ_NOT_USED`: fetched and understood but immaterial to the result;
- `REJECTED`: fetched and excluded, with reason;
- `FETCH_FAILED`: not read, with requested/final URL and exact failure.

Seek counterevidence and record the searched source families. Do not infer
novelty, consensus, or absence from silence. If important content is unavailable,
state what could not be established and how that limits the decision. Cite only
sources actually read and used.
