Ask an ocean or atmosphere question the way you would ask a colleague: "where is the
oxygen minimum zone in the Arabian Sea, and has it changed?", "how well did the forecasts
track this typhoon?", "how biased is this climate model against ERA5?". The agent picks
the data, the definitions and the methods, says which it chose and why, runs the analysis
and shows you the result.

The **ocean** plugin covers CTD casts, Argo floats and gliders, TEOS-10 and water masses,
sections and T-S diagrams, transports and overturning, tides, eddies, SWOT, particles,
marine heatwaves, phytoplankton blooms, mixed-layer budgets, cruise plans and checks of
regional ocean models. The **weather** plugin covers forecasts for a place, ensemble
probabilities, reanalysis thresholds and extremes, atmospheric rivers and cyclone tracks,
cross-sections and soundings with a skew-T chart, forecast scores, AI forecasts and the
evaluation of climate models.

## Turn it on

<Steps>
1. Open **Extensions** and turn on **Ocean analysis**, **Weather**, or both. Turn on
   **Grids** too if you edit model grids (bathymetry, land masks).
2. Or just ask: the agent offers the plugin a question needs, and **Add** puts it in that
   conversation.
</Steps>

## Where the data comes from

Most sources need no account, and the agent uses them directly:

- Argo floats, core and biogeochemical (oxygen, chlorophyll, nitrate).
- Glider deployments in the IOOS Glider DAC.
- NOAA's ERDDAP servers (ocean colour, sea surface temperature and more), NOAA OISST,
  NOAA tide gauges, hourly station observations (GHCN-hourly) and cyclone best tracks
  (IBTrACS).
- ECMWF open data: the IFS and AIFS forecasts and the ensemble.
- ERA5 from its analysis-ready copy on Google Cloud, and CMIP6 model output from the
  Pangeo catalogue.
- SWOT granules can be searched without a login.

Some need an account: Copernicus Marine and the Climate Data Store through the
**Copernicus** connector, and SWOT downloads through the NASA Earthdata login in the
**Ocean analysis** card. You enter an account in the connector's card, never in the
conversation. When a question would be better served by a source you have not set up,
the agent offers it, and goes on with a public source if you choose **Not now**.

Every result carries a provenance record: the sources and their versions, the subset
taken, the method and its parameters, and the data licences.

## Results arrive as views

Ocean and weather results are judged by looking at them, so a tool output that has a
natural view arrives with it in the conversation:

- **Profile stack**: many casts or floats drawn together. Point to a curve to name it,
  click to pick it, and exclude the ones that look wrong; excluded profiles are listed
  below the plot, where one click brings each back.
- **Section**: a variable along a ship track or glider transect against depth, with
  contours. Click a station to select its cast.
- **Map with time**: positions, tracks and gridded fields on a map with a time slider.
- **Grid editor**: select cells of a model grid and choose an edit; each edit is recorded
  by longitude and latitude so it can be replayed onto a regenerated grid.
- **Skew-T chart**: soundings with temperature, dew point and the surface parcel.
- **Route**: a planned route (a cruise, a glider mission, a sampling line) as a line from
  port through its named stops and back, with what it is planned against, such as an
  eddy's track. Drag a stop and choose **Ask to replan**: the agent re-times the plan and
  says what the move changes.

Every map draws latitude and longitude lines with their values, a scale bar, and the names
of a few labelled points, so a view of open ocean still says where it is and how far apart
things are.

## What runs where

Everything runs on the machine that runs CLIO's backend. Two parts need more than the
plugins install by themselves:

- **AI forecast models** (FourCastNet and DLWP through Earth2Studio) need a CUDA GPU to run
  at a useful speed. Without one they run only when you allow it, slowly. ECMWF's own AI
  forecasts (AIFS) come from open data and need no GPU.
- **TempestExtremes**, which tracks blobs and cyclones in gridded fields, is built from
  source and pointed to with `TEMPESTEXTREMES_BIN`.
