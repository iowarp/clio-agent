CLIO searches scholarly sources directly, without a key. Ask for papers the way
you would ask a colleague:

```text
Find three recent papers on lossy compression of scientific
data stored in HDF5, with DOIs.
```

The agent has two literature tools:

| Tool | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Search literature** | Finds papers, preprints, reports and datasets across the sources below. The agent can limit a search to dates, to a field, or to preprints, reviews or datasets. |
| **Look up a work** | Gives one work's details from its DOI, arXiv id, PubMed id or OpenAlex id: where to read it, its references, the works that cite it, and its preprint and published versions. |

To read a paper, the agent opens its readable link, such as the arXiv HTML
page or the open-access full text, with the web tool **Read page**. See
[Search the web](/docs/web-search/).

## Sources

Every literature source that is on is searched together. You do not set an
order.

| Source | What it covers | Free use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OpenAlex | Works across every field, with citations and open-access links | About 100 searches a day per network without a key; looking up one work is free |
| Crossref | DOI metadata for journals and preprints, and the links between them | No daily limit; paced |
| arXiv | Preprints in physics, computing, mathematics and related fields | One request every three seconds |
| ChemRxiv | Chemistry preprints, found through Crossref | Shares Crossref's pace |
| Europe PMC | Life-science literature and preprints, including bioRxiv and medRxiv, with full text where it is open | No key needed |
| OSTI.gov | U.S. Department of Energy reports and papers | No key needed |
| OpenCitations | What a work cites and which works cite it | No key needed |
| DataCite | DOIs for datasets and software | No key needed |
| Semantic Scholar | Works across every field | Only with your key |

ChemRxiv is searched in releases after v0.9.7.

OpenAlex and Crossref answer every search. The other sources join when they
cover the topic: arXiv for physical sciences and computing, ChemRxiv for
chemistry, Europe PMC for life sciences, OSTI.gov for energy, engineering and
earth sciences, and DataCite for datasets. A search for preprints adds arXiv,
ChemRxiv and Europe PMC.

The same work found by several sources appears once. CLIO matches works by DOI,
arXiv id or PubMed id, and lists works found by more sources first. Each result
names the sources that found it, and links a preprint to its published version
when the sources record the link.

When a source is busy or its free allowance is used up, the other sources'
results still arrive, and the agent is told which source was missing and why.
Requests to each source go through one queue for the whole CLIO service, paced to
the source's published limits.

## Change the sources

Open **Settings → Web search** and choose the **Literature** tab.

<Capture client:visible image={await captureImage(literatureSettings)}
  alt="Settings, Web search, Literature tab: OpenAlex with its free allowance and a Get a free OpenAlex key link, then Crossref, arXiv, ChemRxiv, Europe PMC, OSTI.gov, OpenCitations and DataCite, each with an on/off switch"
  caption="Each source shows what it covers, its free use and whether you approved it. Turn a source off to leave it out of every search." />

- **Get a free OpenAlex key** asks OpenAlex to email you a key. It raises the
  daily allowance tenfold. CLIO keeps searching without the key until you open
  the link in the email.
- **Add key** stores a key you already have. Semantic Scholar is searched only
  when it has your key.
- The first literature search asks you once whether CLIO may send queries to the
  literature sources, under every confirmation policy.

## Ask for a literature review

For a review, ask a question that names its scope in **Deep research** mode.
CLIO plans the search, sends several researchers through the literature and the
web, has an independent critic check each claim against the source it quotes,
and writes a report from the verified claims only.

<Steps>

1. Start a new conversation. Open the mode button beside the model (it shows
   **Execute**) and choose **Deep research**.
2. Describe the review: the topic, the questions to answer and any limits.

   ```text
   Write a literature review of machine-learning surrogates
   for wildfire spread prediction since 2019: which modelling
   approaches are used, which datasets they train on, and how
   accurate they are compared with physics-based models.
   ```

3. Approve the search sources when CLIO asks. Follow the researchers in
   **Work** while they run; see [Follow work in progress](/docs/following-work/).
4. Read the report. Its **References** list the papers with their authors, year,
   venue and DOI where the sources provide them.

</Steps>

See [Run deep research](/docs/deep-research/) for how the run works, what the
report contains and how to change its limits.

<Aside type="tip">
Name the field and a date range in your request. The agent turns them into
search filters, which works better than putting years or journal names in the
search words.
</Aside>
