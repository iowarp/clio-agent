Describe the audience, the material to use and the result you need. Review the
finished file, then ask for changes in the same conversation. For help with
opening previews and downloading files, see
[Working with files](/docs/working-with-files/).

## Ask for a report or presentation

Reference your source files and give CLIO a purpose and length. You can also
provide a template or name the sections you need. For example:

> Create a Word report and a PowerPoint presentation from these notes and figures for a fifteen-minute team meeting. Explain the findings and uncertainties, include source captions and speaker notes, and keep the source files unchanged.

Open the result and check the wording, tables, figures and layout. Ask for any
corrections, then review the updated version before sharing it. Download the
editable document if you want to continue in Word or PowerPoint.

### Revise a report

Say what should change and what should stay. This request updates the report's
recommendations while keeping its existing structure:

> Make the next steps request calibration records and a review of the raw readings. Keep the headings, tables, figure and theme. Save a revised Word document and PDF preview, and preserve the original.

## A research briefing

The example uses saved analysis and figures from OPAL 1, a plant-growth research
dataset. Its three-page Word report and eleven-slide meeting deck include speaker
notes, an editable design table and a comparison chart. The report and slides
preserve unresolved treatment labels and an unexplained early break in a vendor
proxy, so the interpretation remains tied to the available evidence.

The initial drafts were revised after visual review. This recording shows review
and navigation of the saved results after those corrections.
