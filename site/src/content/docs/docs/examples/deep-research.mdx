This example follows one deep research run from the question to the report. The
question is a real one from scientific data work: which lossy compressors can you
use on HDF5 data today, and what do papers report about their error bounds and
compression ratios?

You need a working [CLIO installation](/docs/install/), a
[model connection](/docs/connect-a-model/) that can use tools, and at least one
web or literature search source. The run shown here used Claude Sonnet and took
16 minutes. Your run will choose its own directions and sources,
so its wording, counts and layout will differ.

## Ask the question

<Steps>

1. Start a new conversation.

2. Open the mode button beside the model and choose **Deep research**.

   <Capture client:visible image={await captureImage(modeMenu)}
     alt="The composer's mode menu with Execute, Plan and Deep research"
     caption="Deep research applies to the message you send next." />

3. Send the question. It names the compressors of interest, what "available"
   means here and what to report:

   ```text
   What is known about lossy compression for HDF5 scientific data (SZ3, ZFP,
   and similar compressors)? Which ones are available as HDF5 filters today,
   and what error bounds and compression ratios do recent papers report?
   ```

4. Approve the search sources. The first time a researcher searches through a
   source, CLIO asks below the run, naming the direction that wants to search.
   Choose **Allow searches through …** for each source you want the run to use,
   then **Send response**. If you decline one, the run moves to the next source
   you allowed.

   <Capture client:visible image={await captureImage(approval)}
     alt="A running deep research card with four directions, and below it a question: Allow searches through the literature sources? with allow and decline choices and Send response"
     caption="CLIO asks once for each source, while the researchers work. This capture is from an earlier run of the same question." />

</Steps>

## Watch the run

A **Deep research run** card appears in the conversation. The planner splits
the question into named directions, and one researcher works on each, all at
once. In this run the planner chose four directions:

- **HDF5 filter registry status**: which compressors have registered HDF5 filter IDs.
- **SZ and ZFP family capabilities**: what each offers and how its error bounds work.
- **Other lossy compressors and LibPressio**: SPERR, MGARD, TTHRESH and the LibPressio interface.
- **Reported ratios on science datasets**: compression ratios from recent papers.

<Capture client:visible image={await captureImage(running)}
  alt="A running deep research card: Started, Plan, four named researchers in parallel, and a Check step for each, one of them still running"
  caption="The run while it works. Finished steps are outlined, the running one is dashed, and every step is named after its direction." />

Each researcher reports claims, each with the page it read and the exact passage
that supports it. As soon as a researcher finishes, its **Check** step starts.
You can keep using the conversation, or reply to steer the run.

## See how a claim was checked

Choose the full-screen button on the card and select a **Check** step.

<Capture client:visible image={await captureImage(checkStep)}
  alt="The full-screen run graph with a Check step selected, showing how many of that researcher's claims were verified and rejected"
  caption="A Check step reports what it verified and what it rejected for one researcher." />

Two things happen in a check:

- **CLIO checks the quote.** Before any model looks at a claim, CLIO confirms
  that its quoted passage is on the page the researcher read. This is a plain
  text comparison, not a judgement. A quote that is not on the page is rejected
  with that reason.
- **A critic checks the claim.** For each quote that is on the page, the critic
  reads the passage in context and decides whether it supports the claim as
  stated, whether the source is authoritative and current, and whether other
  evidence contradicts it.

In this run, the check of **Reported ratios on science datasets** verified 17
claims and rejected or narrowed 4. Among the rejections were ratios whose
error-bound labels were lost when the page was read, and a ranking across papers
that no single passage supported. Across the whole run, 142 of 143 quoted
passages were found on the page the researcher read; the one that was not was
rejected for that reason. The critics verified 143 claims and rejected 44.

## See what the coordinator decided

Select the **Coordinate** step. The coordinator reads every verified claim
together, from all directions. It records where sources agree, where they
contradict each other and what follows from several of them, and decides whether
the answer needs more research.

<Capture client:visible image={await captureImage(coordinate)}
  alt="The Coordinate step selected, showing the findings it drew across researchers and the follow-ups it chose"
  caption="Coordinate is the one place where follow-up research is decided." />

Here the coordinator made 8 findings across the evidence and sent 3 follow-up
researchers, each on a question none of the first four had answered: **LibPressio
and MGARD filters**, **Independent benchmark ratios**, and **Release dates and
SZ3 modes**. Their claims went through the same checks. A second Coordinate step
after them made 10 findings in all and sent nothing more, because the default
allows one round of follow-ups.

## Read the report

When the run ends, CLIO saves the report as a Markdown file in the workspace's
`deep-research` folder and attaches it to its answer. The answer ends with what
the run could not verify.

<Capture client:visible image={await captureImage(answerEnd)}
  alt="The end of the deep research answer: a table of compression ratios by dataset for SZ3, ZFP and SPERR, caveats and gaps, the reviewer's open issues, and the attached Markdown report"
  caption="The answer ends with its caveats and the reviewer's open issues, then the saved report." />

Choose the file to open the report beside the conversation.

<Capture client:visible image={await captureImage(report)}
  alt="The report open beside the conversation: question and scope, executive answer and findings with a source under each claim"
  caption="Every finding links the page it was checked against." />

This report has 6,078 words. Its findings are grouped into filters and their
IDs, error-bound modes, and reported compression ratios, followed by a section on
where the evidence disagrees or is limited. It says plainly that the papers
measured ratios under different bound conventions, so they do not support a
head-to-head ranking.

A separate reviewer checked the draft against its evidence and sent it back once.
The revised draft still had problems: a garbled sentence comparing SPERR with
SZ3.1, and two statements stronger than their sources. CLIO delivered the report
with those listed under **Open review notes** and repeated them at the end of the
answer, rather than presenting it as clean.

Read these sections first:

- **Executive answer**, for the short answer.
- **Findings**, where each claim links the page its passage came from. A link
  appears only when a researcher or the critic read that page during the run.
- **Rejected claims**, which are not findings. Each one says why it was
  rejected: the quote was not on the page, the page was never read, or the critic
  judged that the passage does not support the claim.
- **Unresolved questions** and **Open review notes**, for what the run could
  not settle.

[Run deep research](/docs/deep-research/#read-the-report) lists every section.

## Check what you rely on

<Aside type="caution">
A verified claim means its quoted passage is on the cited page and a critic
judged that it supports the claim. It does not prove the claim is true. Open the
source of any number you intend to use.
</Aside>

For the claims you depend on, open the link and find the passage. For an
error bound or a compression ratio, check the dataset and the settings it was
measured with: a ratio from one dataset at one error bound rarely transfers to
another.

To go further, reply in the same conversation:

```text
For the compression ratios in the findings, list the dataset, the error bound
and the compressor version each one was measured with, from the cited pages.
```

To change how deep the run goes, see
[Change the depth](/docs/deep-research/#change-the-depth).
