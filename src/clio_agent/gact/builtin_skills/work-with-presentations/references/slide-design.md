# Presentation design

## Plan a narrative that fits the occasion

Identify the audience, meeting purpose and time available. Write the principal
takeaway, then list what the audience must understand to assess it. Give each
slide a purpose and a descriptive title. A research meeting might need context,
data and method, several findings, uncertainty and a decision. Adapt this to the
task; a fixed slide count or a two-slide test deck is not a narrative.

Use the supplied evidence. Retain units, identifiers and exclusions next to
quantitative findings. Put detailed references and presenter context in notes,
but keep caveats that change the conclusion visible. Never invent results,
quotations or decorative metrics to fill a layout.

## Define one visual system

Use the reference deck's conventions when editing. For a new deck, choose an
explicit aspect ratio, fonts available on the execution host, a neutral base,
one primary accent and consistent margins. Start around 30–36 pt for titles and
18–24 pt for body text on a widescreen meeting deck. Source captions can be
smaller, but must remain readable at the intended viewing size.

Use theme defaults or shared builder constants so typography stays coherent.
Align title positions, figure edges and footers. Give content room to breathe
without leaving the slide mostly empty. Design quality comes from hierarchy,
composition and meaningful evidence, not repeated rounded cards or decoration.

## Choose the layout from the content

- A finding with one main figure: use a large, legible figure and a short
  interpretation. Keep the figure's aspect ratio and include its units.
- A comparison: align two views on the same scale or use an editable table or
  chart. Keep colour meaning consistent across slides.
- A method or sequence: use a concise numbered explanation or a clear diagram
  when it communicates an actual relationship.
- An uncertainty or decision: state what is known, what is unresolved and how
  that affects the next action. Avoid a vague closing slogan.

Vary composition when the content needs it; do not force every slide into a
title and a default bullet list. Split dense material across meaningful slides.
For small-multiple charts, check every panel's labels at meeting scale. Separate
panels or regenerate an appropriate figure from the provided data if the full
figure is unreadable. Preserve the complete underlying evidence and explain any
change in grouping or exclusions.

Keep titles, narrative text, tables and charts editable. Source figures can be
images. Do not render an entire slide into a picture to hide layout problems.

## Review what the audience sees

Reopen the PPTX and check slide content, order, notes and required native objects.
Render and inspect every slide. Check composition, text fit, overlap, contrast,
cropping, chart labels and image quality. Bounds checks cannot detect all text
overflow. Revise weak slides as well as broken ones, then regenerate the preview.

Publish the editable deck with its version-bound preview. Distinguish local
LibreOffice rendering from a check in Microsoft PowerPoint.
